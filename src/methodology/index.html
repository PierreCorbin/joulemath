Methodology

How joulemath calculates

Every number on joulemath is your input, live from a public API, or linked to where it came from. Here is exactly how the calculators work.

Live data

  • Difficulty and network hashrate: mempool.space /api/v1/mining/hashrate/3d.
  • Reward per block: total reward (subsidy plus fees) averaged over the last 144 blocks, from mempool.space /api/v1/mining/reward-stats/144. If that call fails we fall back to the 3.125 BTC subsidy alone and say so.
  • BTC price (USD and EUR): mempool.space /api/v1/prices, with CoinGecko as a fallback.

All requests run in your browser when the page loads. Nothing you type is sent to us or anyone else.

Formulas

  • BTC per TH/s per day = 1012 × 86,400 ÷ (difficulty × 232) × reward per block (Bitcoin Wiki: Difficulty).
  • Hashprice = BTC per TH/s per day × BTC price.
  • Mining revenue per day = TH/s × BTC per TH/s per day × (1 − pool fee) × BTC price.
  • Power cost per day = watts ÷ 1,000 × 24 × price per kWh.
  • Breakeven power price = daily revenue ÷ daily kWh.
  • Hardware payback = hardware cost ÷ daily profit, assuming today's difficulty and price stay flat.

Per-kW comparison

The home calculator works per 1 kW of facility power. With PUE p, 1 kW at the meter leaves 1/p kW for IT load. Mining TH/s per kW = 1,000 ÷ p ÷ J/TH. GPUs per kW = 1,000 ÷ p ÷ watts per GPU. GPU revenue per kW per day = GPUs per kW × rate per GPU-hour × 24 × utilization × (1 − marketplace cut). EUR conversion of USD rental rates uses the BTC EUR/USD price ratio.

Full-cost comparison

Per day: net = revenue − power − hardware − hosting. Power = kW × 24 × $/kWh. Hosting = kW × $/kW-month × 12 ÷ 365 (optional, default 0). The home page also shows a "Full numbers" table with these dollar totals.

Scale-free numbers

Mining and GPU setups cost very different amounts for the same power, so raw dollar totals are hard to compare. The headline uses ratios instead:

  • Net / month per $1k invested = net per day × 30.4 ÷ capex × 1,000. In rent mode the base is the rent you pay per month instead of capex.
  • Net margin = net ÷ revenue.
  • Net per kW per day = net per day ÷ facility kW used by that side.

Hosted vs self-run

Hosted (default). Mining: you pay the host an all-in rate per kWh the miners draw, which covers power, cooling, repairs and monitoring, so PUE and a separate hosting fee are not applied. GPUs: wholesale colo is priced per kW of IT load with power billed on top, so cost = IT kW × colo $/kW-month + IT kW × PUE × 24 × electricity price. Self-run: you pay your own power price × PUE, plus any hosting fee you enter.

  • Mining hosting rates: Simple Mining all-in $0.08/kWh for 0 to 499 kW, $0.075 for 500 to 999 kW, $0.07 for 1 to 2 MW, $0.065 Enterprise+, including power, cooling, operations and monitoring (source). Compass Mining offered S21 XP hosting at $0.07/kWh with a 1-unit minimum as a promo, Aug 2026 (source); its M60S tiers run $0.074 to $0.079 (source). Hosts quote 95% (Simple Mining) to 98%+ (Abundant Mines) uptime; the uptime input defaults to 100% so you can set your own.
  • GPU colo $196.25/kW-month: CBRE's North America average asking rate for 250 to 500 kW wholesale colo, power excluded; AI-ready high-density space gets a premium CBRE does not quantify (CBRE, power exclusion per Coloprice Q3 2026). No one publishes a rate for a single GPU or a few kW of dense load; we use this figure for every size and flag it.
  • Marketplace cut 20%: Vast.ai has no host fee since June 2024, but renters pay about 25% above the host's price, so hosts keep about 80% of what renters pay (Vast.ai, Vast.ai). RunPod's Community Cloud kept 24% and is closed to new hosts (RunPod docs). Our rental rate is what renters pay, so the cut comes off it.

Investor profiles

  • Small ($40,000): about 9 S21 XP at the cited $15.81/TH, or 1 GPU at $36,125 (one eighth of the cited 8x H100 server; a lone card is priced differently). Mining at Simple Mining's 0 to 499 kW rate, $0.08/kWh. GPU colo: no published rate at this size, so we use CBRE's and say so. Renters through a marketplace, 20% cut.
  • Growing ($300,000): about 70 S21 XP, or one 8x H100 server ($289,000). Mining at Compass's $0.07/kWh S21 XP promo (Simple Mining's tier for about 280 kW is $0.08). CBRE colo, 20% marketplace cut.
  • Institutional ($5,000,000): MW-scale mining at Simple Mining's Enterprise+ $0.065/kWh. CBRE colo. Renters on direct contracts, so no marketplace cut; that is our assumption, and utilization becomes the term you negotiate.

Budgets are round picks of ours, not data. Utilization stays at 70% (our assumption) in every profile.

Payback and lifetime return

Payback months = capex ÷ ((revenue − power − hosting) × 30.4). Lifetime return per $1 = net per day × 365 × useful life ÷ capex, after hardware is fully paid off. Both assume today's difficulty, BTC price and rental rate stay flat.

Same power vs same budget

Same power (kW): both sides get the same facility kW. With PUE p, TH/s = kW ÷ p × 1,000 ÷ J/TH, and GPUs = kW ÷ p × 1,000 ÷ watts per GPU, rounded down.

Same budget ($): each side spends the same dollars. Buying: TH/s = budget ÷ $/TH, GPUs = budget ÷ price per GPU, rounded down. Renting: the budget is monthly rent spend, so TH/s = budget ÷ (hashprice × (1 + premium) × 30.4) and GPUs = budget ÷ (lease $/hr × 24 × 30.4). The kW each side then uses = TH/s × J/TH ÷ 1,000 × PUE, or GPUs × watts ÷ 1,000 × PUE. "S21 XP" counts assume 270 TH/s per machine.

Buy vs rent

Buy. Mining capex = TH/s × $/TH. GPU capex = GPUs × price per deployed GPU. Hardware per day = capex ÷ (useful life × 365). Useful life defaults (ASIC 3 years, GPU 4 years) are our assumptions.

Rent. Mining: you buy hashrate on a market, so cost per day = hashprice × TH/s × (1 + market premium), and the seller pays power and hosting. Renting hashrate costs more than it mines, so a renter nets below zero. GPUs: you lease GPUs wholesale for every hour and resell them at the rental rate, so cost per day = GPUs × lease $/hr × 24, paid whether or not they are booked, while revenue only comes in at your utilization. Lease at the on-demand price you resell at and you earn about zero or less.

Break-even power price

The power price where mining's net per $1k invested equals the GPU side's, with every other input as you set it. We search $0 to $1/kWh. Below the line, mining earns more per dollar put in; above it, GPUs do. Power is a much bigger share of mining's costs, so mining gains the most from cheap power. Not shown in rent mode, where you pay no power.

Mining uptime

Share of hours the miners run. Revenue and power cost both scale with it. Default 100%. Hardware cost does not, so idle hours still cost you depreciation.

Scenarios

Each button resets the inputs below to the defaults, then changes only what is listed. The URL gets ?scenario= so you can share it. Everything else (live hashprice, GPU prices, PUE) stays the same.

  • Default (?scenario=default): the defaults listed under sources.
  • Cheap power (cheap-power): $0.028/kWh. Riot reported an all-in power cost of 2.8c/kWh for July 2025, net of power credits (source). Industrial hydro in Paraguay runs about $0.037 to $0.050/kWh (Hashrate Index). Power goes for the GPU side too.
  • Used ASICs (used-asics): used Antminer S19 XP (141 TH/s, 21.5 J/TH) at $2.11/TH. That is the $298 median of 34 used eBay sales, May 15 to Aug 14, 2026 (ING Mining). The used S21 (200 TH/s) median was $1,005, about $5.03/TH, on 15 sales (same source). Efficiency is Bitmain's spec as quoted there. Life stays at our 3-year assumption.
  • Curtailment (curtailment): mining uptime 85% and power $0.028/kWh. In July 2025 Riot ran 30.2 EH/s on average out of 35.5 EH/s deployed (85%), while taking part in ERCOT's 4CP and demand response programs, and its all-in power cost after credits was 2.8c/kWh (source). Not all of the gap is curtailment; Riot also cites summer heat. We do not add credits as revenue, they are already in that power price.
  • Low GPU utilization (low-gpu-util): 50%. Data from Alibaba, SenseTime and Microsoft puts production GPU cluster utilization at 25% to 50% (ACM Computing Surveys). Bitdeer reported 41% for its GPU cloud in Jan 2026 (cited by Luminix), which also puts debt-financed breakeven near 70%.

Who can actually do this

Renting GPUs to AI needs a site that tenants accept: redundant power, low-latency fiber, cooling for dense racks (H100 SXM runs up to 700 W per GPU, NVIDIA) and people to keep uptime high. You also need customers. Revenue only comes in when GPUs are booked, which is why utilization moves the result so much.

Mining needs power, airflow or immersion, and an internet link. There are no customers: a pool pays you for hashrate, and the network pays a block reward about every 10 minutes. Miners can switch off on short notice, which is why they can sell flexibility to the grid (see the Riot example above). That flexibility is hard for a GPU host with SLAs.

Taxes (optional)

Tax per day = your rate × max(0, revenue − power − hosting − depreciation). Straight-line spreads depreciation over the useful life. Year-1 expensing (US Section 179 or bonus depreciation style) deducts the whole price up front, which cuts the effective price by your rate; lifetime tax is the same, payback is shorter. We assume you have income to use the deduction. You enter your own rate; we publish none.

Hardware is depreciated much the same way for ASICs and GPUs: straight-line over its life, or in the US possibly expensed up front (IRS Pub 946). Mined coins are income at their market value when you receive them, and selling them later can be a capital gain or loss (IRS Notice 2014-21, IRS virtual currency FAQ). GPU rental is ordinary business income. UK: see HMRC Cryptoassets Manual. Sources accessed Oct 21, 2026. Not tax advice. Rules vary by country; check with an adviser.

Risks

GPU rental prices have been falling as supply grows, and a drop in rate or utilization hits a big fixed hardware bill. Utilization is our assumption; mining runs every hour you have power, rentals only pay when booked. Mining revenue falls as network difficulty rises and moves with the BTC price. Hardware resale value and downtime are not included.

What we leave out

Downtime (except the mining uptime input), curtailment credits as a separate line, hardware resale value, financing costs, tax rules beyond the simple model above and future changes in difficulty or price. GPU power and rental rates are left for you to enter because they vary a lot by server build and platform. Reference prices we quote are what renters pay a marketplace, not what a host nets.
