Methodology

How joulemath calculates

Every number on joulemath is your input, live from a public API, or linked to where it came from. Here is exactly how the calculators work.

Live data

  • Difficulty and network hashrate: mempool.space /api/v1/mining/hashrate/3d.
  • Reward per block: total reward (subsidy plus fees) averaged over the last 144 blocks, from mempool.space /api/v1/mining/reward-stats/144. If that call fails we fall back to the 3.125 BTC subsidy alone and say so.
  • BTC price (USD and EUR): mempool.space /api/v1/prices, with CoinGecko as a fallback.

All requests run in your browser when the page loads. Nothing you type is sent to us or anyone else.

Formulas

  • BTC per TH/s per day = 1012 × 86,400 ÷ (difficulty × 232) × reward per block (Bitcoin Wiki: Difficulty).
  • Hashprice = BTC per TH/s per day × BTC price.
  • Mining revenue per day = TH/s × BTC per TH/s per day × (1 − pool fee) × BTC price.
  • Power cost per day = watts ÷ 1,000 × 24 × price per kWh.
  • Breakeven power price = daily revenue ÷ daily kWh.
  • Hardware payback = hardware cost ÷ daily profit, assuming today's difficulty and price stay flat.

Per-kW comparison

The home calculator works per 1 kW of facility power. With PUE p, 1 kW at the meter leaves 1/p kW for IT load. Mining TH/s per kW = 1,000 ÷ p ÷ J/TH. GPUs per kW = 1,000 ÷ p ÷ watts per GPU. GPU revenue per kW per day = GPUs per kW × rate per GPU-hour × 24 × utilization × (1 − platform overhead). EUR conversion of USD rental rates uses the BTC EUR/USD price ratio.

Full-cost comparison

Per day: net = revenue − power − hardware − hosting. Power = kW × 24 × $/kWh. Hosting = kW × $/kW-month × 12 ÷ 365 (optional, default 0). The home page also shows a "Full numbers" table with these dollar totals.

Scale-free numbers

Mining and GPU setups cost very different amounts for the same power, so raw dollar totals are hard to compare. The headline uses ratios instead:

  • Net / month per $1k invested = net per day × 30.4 ÷ capex × 1,000. In rent mode the base is the rent you pay per month instead of capex.
  • Net margin = net ÷ revenue.
  • Net per kW per day = net per day ÷ facility kW used by that side.

Payback and lifetime return

Payback months = capex ÷ ((revenue − power − hosting) × 30.4). Lifetime return per $1 = net per day × 365 × useful life ÷ capex, after hardware is fully paid off. Both assume today's difficulty, BTC price and rental rate stay flat.

Same power vs same budget

Same power (kW): both sides get the same facility kW. With PUE p, TH/s = kW ÷ p × 1,000 ÷ J/TH, and GPUs = kW ÷ p × 1,000 ÷ watts per GPU, rounded down.

Same budget ($): each side spends the same dollars. Buying: TH/s = budget ÷ $/TH, GPUs = budget ÷ price per GPU, rounded down. Renting: the budget is monthly rent spend, so TH/s = budget ÷ (hashprice × (1 + premium) × 30.4) and GPUs = budget ÷ (lease $/hr × 24 × 30.4). The kW each side then uses = TH/s × J/TH ÷ 1,000 × PUE, or GPUs × watts ÷ 1,000 × PUE. "S21 XP" counts assume 270 TH/s per machine.

Buy vs rent

Buy. Mining capex = TH/s × $/TH. GPU capex = GPUs × price per deployed GPU. Hardware per day = capex ÷ (useful life × 365). Useful life defaults (ASIC 3 years, GPU 4 years) are our assumptions.

Rent. Mining: you buy hashrate on a market, so cost per day = hashprice × TH/s × (1 + market premium), and the seller pays power and hosting. Renting hashrate costs more than it mines, so a renter nets below zero. GPUs: you lease GPUs wholesale for every hour and resell them at the rental rate, so cost per day = GPUs × lease $/hr × 24, paid whether or not they are booked, while revenue only comes in at your utilization. Lease at the on-demand price you resell at and you earn about zero or less.

Taxes (optional)

Tax per day = your rate × max(0, revenue − power − hosting − depreciation). Straight-line spreads depreciation over the useful life. Year-1 expensing (US Section 179 or bonus depreciation style) deducts the whole price up front, which cuts the effective price by your rate; lifetime tax is the same, payback is shorter. We assume you have income to use the deduction. You enter your own rate; we publish none.

Hardware is depreciated much the same way for ASICs and GPUs: straight-line over its life, or in the US possibly expensed up front (IRS Pub 946). Mined coins are income at their market value when you receive them, and selling them later can be a capital gain or loss (IRS Notice 2014-21, IRS virtual currency FAQ). GPU rental is ordinary business income. UK: see HMRC Cryptoassets Manual. Sources accessed Oct 21, 2026. Not tax advice. Rules vary by country; check with an adviser.

Risks

GPU rental prices have been falling as supply grows, and a drop in rate or utilization hits a big fixed hardware bill. Utilization is our assumption; mining runs every hour you have power, rentals only pay when booked. Mining revenue falls as network difficulty rises and moves with the BTC price. Hardware resale value and downtime are not included.

What we leave out

Downtime, curtailment revenue, hardware resale value, financing costs, tax rules beyond the simple model above and future changes in difficulty or price. GPU power and rental rates are left for you to enter because they vary a lot by server build and platform. Reference prices we quote are what renters pay a marketplace, not what a host nets.
