About joulemath

joulemath is a small set of calculators and explainers about one question: what is a kilowatt-hour worth when you turn it into compute? Right now that means Bitcoin mining and renting GPUs to AI workloads.

Who runs it

joulemath is built and run by Pierre Corbin. The site's code, its build scripts and the raw data reads behind the hand-checked prices are public in the PierreCorbin/joulemath repository on GitHub, so anyone can see how each number is produced and when it changed.

We are not a hardware seller or a GPU cloud, and we do not run a mining pool. Data pulls and first drafts are partly automated. Every figure is checked against the source linked next to it before it ships, and a page that cannot be sourced is not published.

Rules we stick to

  • Every number on the site is either your input, live from a public API, or linked to where it came from, with the date we read it.
  • If we cannot source something, we leave it blank and say so. We do not estimate a missing price.
  • Where a figure is our own arithmetic (break-even power price, payback, cost per BTC), the page says so and the formula is on the methodology page.
  • Thin samples are labelled. If a marketplace had three offers, the page says three offers.

Where the data comes from

  • Bitcoin network: the free mempool.space API for difficulty, block rewards, fees, the next difficulty adjustment and BTC price, with CoinGecko as a price fallback.
  • ASIC specs and prices: Bitmain spec sheets and the MicroBT shop for specs and list prices, and the ING Mining used ASIC sold price index (eBay sales) for used prices. Each model page links its own sources.
  • GPU rent: the public offers API of Vast.ai, published price lists from RunPod and Lambda, Clore's public API, Salad's published earning rates and the Ornn index of executed H100 and H200 trades. The weekly GPU rent index keeps each week's reads.
  • Power prices: official tariffs and statistics such as the EIA, Hydro-Quebec and DEWA, plus named industry reports where no tariff is published. Each power price page lists its sources.

How often pages change

Live network numbers (hashprice, difficulty, BTC price) are recomputed when the site rebuilds, once a day, and in your browser on the calculators. Hand-read prices are re-read on a schedule: GPU rent weekly, used ASIC prices and power tariffs when the source updates. The "Page updated" date on each page moves only when its words or hand-read data change, not on the daily rebuild. Older blog posts are dated snapshots and are marked as archives when a newer page replaces them.

Found a mistake or a better source? Open an issue on the GitHub repository and we will fix it and note the change.

Privacy

If you ask for the weekly email, we store your email address, the page the form was on, the consent text you accepted, and the times you requested it, confirmed it, or unsubscribed. We send a confirmation link first. You are not subscribed until you open it. We do not sell or share the address. Unsubscribe stops the mail and keeps the address suppressed so it is not added again without a new confirmation. A short-lived hash of your IP address is used only to limit signup attempts, and it is not stored with your subscription. We do not store Bitcoin payout addresses or Vast.ai machine IDs on our server. Those stay in your browser.

We also count a few anonymous things, like clicks on outbound links and which preset or toggle people pick, as plain daily totals. No cookies, no IP addresses, nothing tied to you. A signup attempt counted in the browser is not a subscriber. Confirmed subscribers are counted only after the confirmation link is used. If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, we skip the anonymous counts entirely.
